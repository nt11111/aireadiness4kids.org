## Timing for a 45-60 minute session

| Minutes | What happens | Format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0-3 | Pre-check, read aloud | Whole group |
| 3-9 | Step 1: the made-up reading list | Whole group |
| 9-19 | Step 2 and the three-bucket sort | Small groups |
| 19-29 | Step 3 Play it: Sure, but wrong | Whole group, then pairs |
| 29-36 | Step 4 scenario vote | Whole group |
| 36-41 | Step 5 quick check | Whole group |
| 41-48 | Step 6: What ARK thinks, then reflect | Individual, then pairs |
| 48-52 | Step 7 recap and post-check | Whole group |

## Before you start

- Every activity is unplugged or led by an adult. Learners do not use AI tools themselves.
- Keep the tone light. The goal is checking, not fear of AI.
- The self-driving car example is for grades 3-5. For younger groups, skip it.
- "What ARK thinks" is an opinion. Say so out loud, and let kids disagree.

## Discussion prompts

Use the two in Step 6.

## Answer notes

Quick check: 1 B, 2 C, 3 A. Pre/post: 1 B, 2 B, 3 B. In Step 3, true: spiders and octopus. False: the sun (a star) and penguins (most live in the Southern Hemisphere). In the sort, accept any answer a learner can explain with "what happens if it's wrong?"
