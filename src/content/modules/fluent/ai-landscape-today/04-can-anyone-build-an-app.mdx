You have probably seen the claim: "Anyone can build an app with AI now." <Vocab term="vibe coding" /> means building software by describing what you want in plain language and letting an AI tool write the code. Collins Dictionary named it the 2025 Word of the Year. Here you run the last Hype Detector question: can I test it myself?

Before you start, write down how many minutes you think this will take.

<Callout kind="fact">
In a 2025 study by the research group METR, 16 experienced programmers expected AI to speed them up by 24%. They took 19% longer, and afterward still believed AI had saved them 20%. METR's 2026 follow-up suggests newer tools probably help more, but says its data can't yet show how much.
</Callout>

<TryIt offline={"No AI tool? Write the exact prompt you would give, list five tests, and predict which ones a first version would fail. Your facilitator can show a sample result."} warning={"Never type your name, school, or anything personal into an AI tool. Many AI tools require users to be 13 or older, and some set a higher age, so check the rules first."}>
Use an AI tool your school allows, or the ARK practice tool. Ask it to build a one-page flashcard quiz: you type terms and definitions, and it quizzes you in random order.

Then try to break it. Enter no cards. Enter one card. Paste a definition 500 words long. Use an apostrophe or an emoji. Refresh the page. Note what works and what fails.
</TryIt>

Now judge the claim in three sentences:

1. What the tool got right on the first try.
2. Where it fell short, and why you think so. Was it a hard part or an easy-looking one?
3. Your verdict: is "anyone can build an app" real, stretched, or a forecast?

Compare your time with your guess. Then look for a pattern: did it work on ordinary input and break on cases you never described? The model writes likely code for what you asked, not for what you forgot to ask.
