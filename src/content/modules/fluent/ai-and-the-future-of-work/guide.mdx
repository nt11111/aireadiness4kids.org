## Timing for a 75-minute class

For 60 minutes, skip the second reflection prompt and shorten the debate. For 90 minutes, have pairs present their graded tasks and defend their grades.

| Minutes | What happens | Format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0-3 | Pre-check | Individual |
| 3-13 | Step 1 job lists | Individual, then pairs |
| 13-25 | Step 2: sort the four kinds of numbers by what they measured | Small groups |
| 25-37 | Step 3 and the 2040 question | Whole class |
| 37-50 | Step 4 Try it | Pairs |
| 50-58 | Step 5 internship scenario | Pairs |
| 58-63 | Step 6 quick check | Individual |
| 63-71 | Step 7: ARK's view, reflect, then the interns debate | Individual, then whole class |
| 71-75 | Step 8 recap and post-check | Whole class |

## Before you start

- Some students have family members who have lost work or worry about losing it. Keep examples about tasks and systems, and never ask whose parent works where.
- Avoid both doom and hype. The honest answer for 2026 is that measured effects are small overall, with early signs for young workers in some jobs.
- Labor numbers change often. Check the Stanford, Yale, and Anthropic pages for newer updates before you teach.
- ARK's view is an opinion, labeled as one. Invite students to argue with it. No grade depends on agreeing.

## Discussion prompts

Use the deck's interns debate: CEO, interns, manager, customer. Then ask the class which choice in Step 5 they would actually make.

## Answer notes

Quick check: 1 B, 2 D, 3 B, 4 A, 5 B. Pre/post: 1 B, 2 A, 3 C. If learners choose A on quick check question 5, point out that the AI group still finished the task. The gap was in what they could do afterward.
