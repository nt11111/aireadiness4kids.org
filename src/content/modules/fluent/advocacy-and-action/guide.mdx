## Timing for a 75-minute class

For 60 minutes, assign the action plan as homework and present next session. For 90 minutes, run full three-minute public comment practice for volunteers.

| Minutes | What happens | Format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0-3 | Pre-check | Individual |
| 3-10 | Step 1: what did these people do first? | Whole class |
| 10-20 | Step 2 and the strong-or-weak sort | Pairs |
| 20-30 | Step 3: write one theory of change together, then test the Kill Switch Act's | Whole class |
| 30-42 | Step 4 Try it | Pairs |
| 42-50 | Step 5 school board scenario | Pairs |
| 50-54 | Step 6 quick check | Individual |
| 54-70 | Step 7: ARK's view, then action plans and peer feedback | Individual, then pairs |
| 70-75 | Step 8 recap, post-check, and certificate | Whole class |

## Before you start

- Students choose their own issues. Help them narrow broad issues to one decision-maker and one ask.
- Some issues touch politics. Keep the focus on the method: the ask, the evidence, and the theory of change. Don't tell students which side to take. The Kill Switch Act has sponsors from both parties, and students can argue for or against it.
- ARK's view is an opinion, labeled as one. Invite students to argue with it. No grade depends on agreeing.
- If a student's issue involves harm to a real person, such as fake images, steer the plan toward policy and support systems, never toward naming anyone.

## Discussion prompts

Use the deck's "In or Out?" debate (inside, outside, build, teach) and the "What bugs you?" poll to help students pick issues.

## Answer notes

Quick check: 1 B, 2 C, 3 B, 4 C. Pre/post: 1 B, 2 A, 3 B. If learners pick A on quick check question 2, point out that a fact is evidence, not an ask.
