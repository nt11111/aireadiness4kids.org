  prompt: "How do you open?"
  options:
    - id: a
      text: "A short story about a real situation at your school, shared with permission and without names."
      feedback: "Strong. A story gives the board something to picture, and it earns attention for your ask. Follow it with your evidence and your ask."
    - id: b
      text: "A statistic."
      feedback: "Evidence matters, but a number alone gives the board nothing to picture. Put it second, right after a story."
    - id: c
      text: "Your ask, in one sentence."
      feedback: "Also strong. The board knows from the first moment what you want. Repeat it at the end, so it is the last thing they hear."
    - id: d
      text: "A criticism of the board."
      feedback: "You may be right, but people who feel attacked stop listening. Criticize the policy, not the people."
---

Your school board is voting on a new AI policy. You signed up for three minutes of public comment. (Rules for public comment vary by district, so check yours.)

<Scenario />

From the deck's "Present Like a Pro": open with the story, not the statistics. Make one ask per audience, and say it twice. End with the first step, not a thank-you.
