A plan gets stronger when someone tries to knock it down. Here an AI tool plays the skeptic.

<TryIt offline={"No AI tool? A partner plays the board member and pushes back for three minutes."} warning={"Use a role, like \"board member,\" not a real person's name. Don't type personal information into an AI tool."}>
Write your ask in one sentence. Use an AI tool your school allows, or the ARK practice tool. Give it this prompt: "You are a busy, skeptical school board member. Give me the three strongest reasons to say no to this request: [your ask]." Then ask: "What would change your mind?"

Sort each objection into one of three piles:

- **Real:** fix your plan.
- **Answerable:** add evidence or a story to your brief.
- **Wrong:** the AI got a fact or a rule wrong. You learned why it can in Module 1. Check before you trust it.
</TryIt>

The AI's objections are input, not a verdict. You decide what to change. Then rewrite your ask so it survives the best objection.
