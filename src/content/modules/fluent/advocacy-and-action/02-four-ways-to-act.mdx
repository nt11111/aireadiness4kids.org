Advocacy comes in four main forms. Pick the one that fits your audience.

- **Write:** op-eds, policy briefs, and public comments. Best for decision-makers and the public.
- **Organize:** clubs, petitions, and coalitions. Best when numbers matter.
- **Build:** tools that show a better way. Best when people need to see it working.
- **Speak:** testimony, talks, and meetings. Best when you can get in the room.

An <Vocab term="op-ed" /> is an opinion article by someone outside a news outlet's staff. A <Vocab term="policy brief" /> goes straight to a decision-maker. A good brief has four parts:

1. **The problem:** one sentence, with one real example.
2. **The stakes:** who is harmed, and how much.
3. **The evidence:** two or three solid sources beat twenty weak ones.
4. **The ask:** a specific action, by a specific person, with a date.

<Vocab term="the ask" /> is the part most people get wrong. "Someone should do something" is not an ask.

<Sort />

Recall the public comment rule from Module 2: it is not a vote. One well-reasoned comment with evidence can count for more than a hundred copies of the same message.
