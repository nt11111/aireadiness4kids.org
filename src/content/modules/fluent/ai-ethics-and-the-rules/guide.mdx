## Timing for a 75-minute class

For 60 minutes, cut the face-scan debate. For 90 minutes, run the incident-rule scenario as a full mock hearing.

| Minutes | What happens | Format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0-3 | Pre-check | Individual |
| 3-10 | Step 1: vote on who should pay before the reveal | Whole class |
| 10-22 | Step 2: fill in the three-approaches table together | Whole class |
| 22-32 | Step 3: Collingridge examples, then the early-or-late question | Pairs |
| 32-47 | Step 4 Try it | Pairs |
| 47-60 | Step 5 incident-rule hearing, with roles: AI lab, the company that was hacked, state regulator, member of the public | Groups of four |
| 60-64 | Step 6 quick check | Individual |
| 64-72 | Step 7: ARK's view, reflect, then the trust corner vote | Individual, then whole class |
| 72-75 | Step 8 recap and post-check | Whole class |

## Before you start

- Policy facts in this module change often. Read the Check these notes and update dates before you teach.
- Keep the focus on how rules work, not on which party is right. When students raise current politics, ask what evidence each side would need. The Kill Switch Act has sponsors from both parties.
- ARK's view is an opinion, labeled as one. Invite students to argue with it. No grade depends on agreeing.
- The Dutch case involved discrimination by nationality. Some students may have families affected by similar systems. Let anyone pass on discussion.

## Discussion prompts

Use the deck's face-scan debate: principal, students, parents, city council. Then ask who should decide, and when.

## Answer notes

Quick check: 1 C, 2 D, 3 A, 4 B, 5 B. Pre/post: 1 A, 2 C, 3 B. If learners pick B on quick check question 3, ask whether laws always lag. Point to the EU sandbox: some rules are designed to move early.
