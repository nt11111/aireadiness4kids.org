topics:
  - "What is bias, and how does it get into training data?"
  - "Real cases: facial recognition failures, hiring algorithms, predictive policing"
  - "Who is most affected by biased AI?"
  - "What can be done to reduce AI bias?"
objectives:
  - "Explain what bias means and how it can get into an AI tool"
  - "Name three places bias can enter: the data, the goal people choose, and how a tool is tested and used"
  - "Recognize who can be harmed when an AI tool works better for some people than others"
  - "Suggest practical ways to catch and reduce bias before a tool is used"
vocabulary:
  - term: "bias"
    def: "A pattern that unfairly favors or works against some people or groups."
  - term: "training data"
    def: "The examples an AI tool learns from, like labeled photos, emails, or past decisions."
  - term: "algorithm"
    def: "A set of step-by-step instructions that a computer follows."
  - term: "feedback loop"
    def: "When a tool's results become its future data, so a pattern keeps repeating and can grow."
  - term: "audit"
    def: "A careful check of a system, often by outside experts, to see whether it works fairly for everyone."
