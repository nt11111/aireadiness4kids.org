Before you share a clip, use three moves. They work on images, video, and audio.

Stop. How does the clip make you feel? A strong feeling is a signal to pause before you share.

Check the source. Who posted it first, and what is their history? Who gains if you believe it?

Find a second source. Does a source you trust confirm it? If not, treat it as unconfirmed.

<Figure src="/lessons/literate/deepfakes-and-misinformation/three-moves.svg" alt={"Diagram of three steps in order: stop, check the source, find a second source."} width={440} height={404} />

The third move uses <Vocab term="lateral reading" />: leaving a page to see what other trusted sources say about it. Open a new tab and search for the claim. Don't keep reading the page that gave it to you.

For a video, take a screenshot and run a reverse image search. It can lead you to the original. For audio, search for the quote and see who else reports it.

<Callout kind="tip">
Try it on Ethan's clip. Who posted it first? Do other trusted sources, like Ethan's own account or the school newspaper, say he said it?
</Callout>

<Callout kind="warning">
Odd details can make you pause, but a clean clip proves nothing. If you can't confirm it, don't pass it on.
</Callout>
