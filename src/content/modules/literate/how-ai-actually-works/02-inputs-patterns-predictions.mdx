Here's a simple way to think about machine learning. It has four parts.

Training data. People collect huge numbers of examples. For a recommender like Instagram, that means videos people watched, skipped, liked, or saved. This is the <Vocab term="training data" />.

Training. An <Vocab term="algorithm" /> goes through the examples and adjusts itself to find patterns. It might notice that people who finish cooking videos often watch more of them.

The model. After training, you have a <Vocab term="model" />. Give it a new video and a viewer, the input. It gives back a prediction: how likely you are to watch, like, or save it.

Feedback. When you tap "not interested" or watch something all the way through, that becomes new data. This is a <Vocab term="feedback loop" />.

An algorithm is step-by-step instructions, like a recipe. Machine learning is one way to build AI: it learns patterns from examples instead of following typed-in rules. AI is the bigger category: systems built to do tasks that usually need human thinking.

<Callout kind="fact">
A model finds patterns. It doesn't know what a video actually is.
</Callout>
