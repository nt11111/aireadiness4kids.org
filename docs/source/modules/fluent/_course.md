# AI Fluent (9-12)

**Formerly:** AI Architects. **Writer:** Neil. **Peer checker:** Viren. **Theme:** Agency, Policy & Action.[ Slides folder](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1olHGh5ks8SIQfz5mnZci9R3pCgBxlcxi)

**Course description (the site's text, with the new course name):** The AI Fluent course is designed for students ready to think seriously about AI's role in society, and their own role in shaping it. Modules move from understanding to agency and action.

**What learners get from it (on the site now):**

- What today's generative AI can and can't do, beyond the marketing claims
- Who makes the rules for AI, and where accountability falls short
- How algorithmic systems can reinforce inequity, and how researchers audit them
- How AI is reshaping work, and which human skills matter most
- How to design an AI product responsibly, including red-teaming for misuse
- How to turn your ideas into advocacy with a one-page action plan

**Voice:** Ages 14 to 18. Up to 350 words per step and 22 words per sentence. Reads like a good intro college course. Real cases with sources, and open about what is unsettled. A finished slide deck exists for each module, so start from its slides.

**What that changed in every module (October 13):**

- **One hard idea per module.** Each module teaches one idea that stays true as products and laws change. Current products, laws, and numbers appear only as dated examples ("as of 2026") that can be swapped out.
- **Eight steps instead of six.** Hook, Core idea, The hard idea, Try it, Scenario, Quick check, Reflect, Recap. The guide allows up to eight.
- **A "Try it" step in every module.** Each uses a new [TRY] tag and works three ways: with an AI tool the school allows, with the ARK practice tool once it exists, or with a no-AI version for class.
- **YOUR TURN notes stay** wherever Neil's own school, district, or experience would make a step stronger.

**What that changed (October 14):**

- **Recent events in every module.** Each module header lists the dated 2025-2026 events it uses, with "last checked October 14, 2026". The hard ideas stay the same. The events are evidence for them, and get rechecked each term.

- **One ARK's view per module,** in the Reflect step, with the best case against it (guide section 7b). Learners are asked whether they agree, and what would change their mind.
