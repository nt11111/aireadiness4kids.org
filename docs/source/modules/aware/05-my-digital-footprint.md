## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition for this grade band |
| --- | --- |
| digital footprint | The trail of things you do online. |
| personal information | Facts about you, like your full name, address, school, or birthday. |
| private | Just for you and your family. |
| password | A secret word that keeps your things locked. |

## Step 1: Hook (explainer, 5 min, free preview)

**Title:** Footprints in the sand

When you walk on a beach, you leave footprints. Someone could follow them.

Devices keep a trail too. You leave it when you tap, type, or talk. That trail is your [VOCAB digital footprint].

Footprints in sand wash away. Many online footprints do not.

[CALLOUT think] What footprints might you leave today? Think about games, videos, and talking toys.

In this lesson you will find out what AI tools keep. You will also learn how to keep your footprint small.

## Step 2: Core idea (explainer, 7 min)

**Title:** What AI tools keep

What you type or say to an app can be saved. It can be used later.

Some AI toys record kids' voices. Many chatbots learn from what people type to them.

Some facts are [VOCAB personal information]. Your full name, your address, your school, and your birthday are personal. Keep them [VOCAB private]. Your [VOCAB password] is always private.

[SORT] Share or keep private? | OK to share; Keep private; Ask a grown-up

- Your favorite color -> OK to share -> It does not tell anyone who you are.
- Your home address -> Keep private -> It tells people where you live.
- Your password -> Keep private -> It unlocks your things.
- Your school's name -> Keep private -> It helps strangers find you.
- Your pet's name -> Ask a grown-up -> Some people use it in passwords.
- A photo of your face -> Ask a grown-up -> Photos can be copied and shared.

## Step 3: Play it (activity, 10 min)

**Title:** The footprint trail

This game is unplugged. Each player gets five paper footprints. On each one, a grown-up writes a small made-up fact. For example: "likes soccer" or "has a dog named Max." Or: "walks past the big library."

Lay the footprints in a trail. Then a "detective" picks up the trail.

Can the detective guess things about the made-up kid? Where they live? What their password might be?

[CALLOUT think] One footprint told the detective very little. What did all five tell them together?

Small facts add up. That is why you share the least you can.

[CALLOUT tip] **Level up (grades 3-5).** A US law called COPPA protects kids under 13. Many apps must ask a parent before collecting a child's information. The rules got stronger in 2025.

## Step 4: Scenario (scenario, 6 min)

**Title:** The friendly chatbot

You are playing a game. A friendly chatbot asks, "What school do you go to?" (This game is made up.)

[SCENARIO] What would you do?

- **A. Answer it.** Feedback: The bot sounds friendly, but your school is personal information. Once you share it, you can't take it back.
- **B. Make something up.** Feedback: That keeps your real school private. But the bot might keep asking. A grown-up can help you decide what to do.
- **C. Close the game.** Feedback: That keeps you safe right away. Tell a grown-up what happened, too.
- **D. Ask a grown-up first.** Feedback: The best choice. A grown-up can check the game and help you stay safe.

## Step 6: Reflect (reflect, 5 min)

**Title:** My footprint

**What ARK thinks:** Share the least you can. If an app does not need it, it does not get it.

**Some people say:** Sharing more helps apps work better for you.

[REFLECT] What do you think? What would you never share with an app?

[REFLECT] What would you like your footprint to say about you?

This is just for you. Nobody else will see it.

[DISCUSS]

- What is one rule your family could make about sharing online?
- Why might a toy or a game want to know about you?

## Facilitator guide

**Materials:** Paper footprints, five per player, with made-up facts written on them. Signs for the three sort buckets.

**Timing for a 45-60 minute session:**

| Minutes | What happens | Format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0-3 | Pre-check, read aloud | Whole group |
| 3-8 | Step 1: footprints in the sand | Whole group |
| 8-18 | Step 2 and the share-or-private sort | Small groups |
| 18-28 | Step 3 Play it: The footprint trail | Small groups |
| 28-35 | Step 4: the friendly chatbot | Whole group |
| 35-39 | Step 5 quick check | Whole group |
| 39-47 | Step 6: What ARK thinks, then reflect | Individual, then pairs |
| 47-52 | Step 7 recap, post-check, and certificate | Whole group |

**Before you start:**

- Use only made-up facts on the footprints. Never ask learners for real information about themselves.
- Learners do not use AI tools or AI toys in this session.
- The school-name card can spark debate. Some families share it freely. Talk about who can see it.
- "What ARK thinks" is an opinion. Say so out loud, and let kids disagree.

**Discussion prompts:** Use the two in Step 6.

**Answer notes:** Quick check: 1 B, 2 B, 3 B. Pre/post: 1 A, 2 B, 3 B. In the sort, accept "Ask a grown-up" for the school name, too.
