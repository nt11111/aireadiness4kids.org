# AI Aware (K-5)

**Formerly:** AI Explorers. **Writer:** Viren. **Peer checker:** Rishi. **Theme:** Discovery & Awareness.[ Slides folder](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1RYQ5lLN-0gKUg4ef3VwQKcWJTPnEaKiR)

**Course description (the site's text, with the new course name):** The AI Aware course introduces young learners to the idea that AI exists in the world around them, how it helps and sometimes confuses us, and what it means to be a thoughtful user of technology.

**What learners get from it (on the site now):**

- What AI is, and how it differs from robots and ordinary computers
- Why AI makes mistakes, and when to double-check its answers
- How to give AI clear instructions and check what it says
- How AI-made images and messages can affect feelings, and how to stay kind online
- What a digital footprint is, and how to keep personal information private

**Voice:** Ages 5 to 10, often read aloud. Up to 150 words per step and 12 words per sentence. One idea per step. Activities are unplugged or led by an adult, since most AI tools require users to be 13 or older.

**What changed on October 14:**

- **Full starter drafts in all five tabs.** Claude wrote them from the October 2 outlines. Nobody has reviewed them. Viren decides what stays.
- **A Play it step in every module.** An unplugged game after Core idea, where kids act as the AI. No device needed.
- **Level up boxes.** A harder idea for grades 3-5, up to 50 words. K-2 groups skip them.
- **Real cases, told for kids.** A fake summer reading list, robotaxis and school buses, AI toys, and the 2025 update to the kids' privacy law. Each has a source.
- **What ARK thinks.** One opinion per module, with "Some people say" on the other side, and the question "What do you think?"
- **Why:** the outlines were mostly "ask a grown-up" rules. That works for K-2, but it was too easy for grades 3-5. Kids this age can predict, test, and argue, so the drafts ask them to.
