## Step 1: Hook (explainer, 5 min, free preview)

**Title:** The robot sandwich

Your grown-up pretends to be a robot. The robot does exactly what you say. Nothing more.

You say, "Put jam on the bread."

The robot picks up the whole jar. It sets the jar on top of the bread!

[CALLOUT think] What went wrong? What should you have said?

The robot did what you said, not what you meant. AI tools can be like that too.

The words you give an AI are called a [VOCAB prompt]. A good prompt is a clear [VOCAB instruction]. It says exactly what you want.

## Step 2: Core idea (explainer, 7 min)

**Title:** Good prompts have details

A fuzzy prompt gets a fuzzy answer. A clear prompt gets a better one.

Fuzzy: "Tell me about dogs."

Clear: "Tell me three fun facts about puppies for a first grader."

The clear prompt says three things:

- **Who** it is for: a first grader.
- **What** you want: fun facts about puppies.
- **How much:** three facts.

[SORT] Clear or fuzzy? | Clear; Fuzzy

- "Write a story." -> Fuzzy -> About what? For whom? How long?
- "Write a short story about a brave cat for my little brother." -> Clear -> It says what, who, and how long.
- "Help with math." -> Fuzzy -> Which math? What kind of help?
- "Show me two ways to add 8 and 5." -> Clear -> It says exactly what you want.

Even a clear prompt can get a wrong answer. So check it with a second [VOCAB source]. A book or a teacher works.

## Step 3: Play it (activity, 10 min)

**Title:** Prompt and check

This game is unplugged or led by your grown-up. Learners do not type into an AI tool.

Your grown-up is the AI. Give them a fuzzy prompt, like "Draw an animal." Your grown-up draws anything at all.

Now try: "Draw a happy cat in a red hat." Is the drawing closer?

Next, your grown-up gives an "AI answer" to a question. Find a book that checks it. Was the answer right?

[CALLOUT think] Which worked better, the fuzzy prompt or the clear one? Why?

[CALLOUT tip] **Level up (grades 3-5).** Older students practiced math with a chatbot. When it was taken away, they did worse on a test. Students whose chatbot gave hints mostly did not. Why might hints help more?

**Sources:**

- High school students practicing math with GPT-4 (Bastani et al., PNAS, 2025) ->[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40560616/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40560616/)

## Step 4: Scenario (scenario, 6 min)

**Title:** Ask AI or ask a person?

Maya has three questions. (Maya is made up.)

- How do you spell "elephant"?
- Why does my best friend seem sad today?
- Is the berry in my yard safe to eat?

[SCENARIO] Which question should Maya take to a person first?

- **A. The spelling question.** Feedback: An AI tool or a dictionary can help with spelling. This one is fine to look up.
- **B. The question about her friend.** Feedback: Yes. Feelings are for people. Her friend, a parent, or a teacher can really help.
- **C. The berry question.** Feedback: Yes. Safety questions go to a trusted grown-up. A wrong answer could make her sick.
- **D. None of them. AI can answer all three.** Feedback: AI can't know how her friend feels. It also can't see the berry. People are better for these.

Feelings and safety questions go to a trusted grown-up first.

## Step 6: Reflect (reflect, 5 min)

**Title:** One for AI, one for a person

**What ARK thinks:** Use AI to think more, not less. If AI does all the thinking, your brain misses the practice.

**Some people say:** AI saves time. Let it do the boring parts.

[REFLECT] What do you think? When should you do the thinking yourself?

[REFLECT] Name one thing you would ask an AI. Name one thing only for a person.

This is just for you. Nobody else will see it.

[DISCUSS]

## Facilitator guide

**Materials:** Bread, a plastic knife, and a jar of jam for the robot sandwich, or paper for a draw-what-I-say version. Paper and crayons for Step 3. One children's book you can use to check an answer.

**Timing for a 45-60 minute session:**

| Minutes | What happens | Format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0-3 | Pre-check, read aloud | Whole group |
| 3-10 | Step 1: the robot sandwich | Whole group |
| 10-19 | Step 2 and the clear-or-fuzzy sort | Pairs |
| 19-29 | Step 3 Play it: Prompt and check | Whole group |
| 29-36 | Step 4: Maya's three questions | Whole group |
| 36-40 | Step 5 quick check | Whole group |
| 40-47 | Step 6: What ARK thinks, then reflect | Individual, then pairs |
| 47-52 | Step 7 recap and post-check | Whole group |

**Before you start:**

- Learners do not use AI tools themselves. If you show one, you type, and you check its age rules first.
- Check food allergies before the sandwich activity, or use the paper version.
- The Level up study was with high school students. Say so, and keep it simple.
- "What ARK thinks" is an opinion. Say so out loud, and let kids disagree.

**Discussion prompts:** Use the two in Step 6.

**Answer notes:** Quick check: 1 B, 2 B, 3 B. Pre/post: 1 A, 2 B, 3 B. In Step 4, B and C both go to a person first. A is fine to look up.
