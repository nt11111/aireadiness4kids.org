## Step 1: Hook (explainer, 5 min, free preview)

**Title:** Oops, AI!

In 2025, some newspapers printed a summer reading list. A writer used AI to help make it.

There was one big problem. Most of the books were not real!

Only 5 of the 15 books on the list were real books. The AI made up the rest. It even made up what they were about.

The list looked sure of itself. It was still wrong.

That is called a [VOCAB mistake]: when an AI makes a wrong guess.

[CALLOUT think] How could the writer have caught the made-up books?

Helpful tools still get things wrong. In this lesson you will learn why. You will also learn what to do about it.

## Step 2: Core idea (explainer, 7 min)

**Title:** Why AI gets it wrong

AI learns from examples. So its mistakes often come from its examples.

**Reason 1: too few examples.** An AI that saw only red apples may miss a green one.

**Reason 2: wrong examples.** If the examples were wrong, the AI learns wrong things. Some examples leave people out. Then the AI may not be [VOCAB fair] to them.

Here is a tricky part. AI can sound very sure, even when it is wrong. Sounding sure is not proof.

So when should you [VOCAB double-check]? It depends on what happens if the answer is wrong.

[SORT] What should you do? | Fine to use; Double-check; Ask a grown-up

- A song the AI picked for you -> Fine to use -> If you don't like it, just skip it.
- A fact for your school poster -> Double-check -> Others will read it, so check a book.
- How to spell a word -> Double-check -> Look it up to be sure.
- A question about medicine -> Ask a grown-up -> Health questions go to people you trust.
- Which berry is safe to eat -> Ask a grown-up -> A wrong answer could hurt you.
- A funny name for your pet -> Fine to use -> Nothing bad happens if it's silly.

## Step 3: Play it (activity, 10 min)

**Title:** Sure, but wrong

This game is unplugged. Your grown-up reads four facts. They use a very sure robot voice. Some are true. Some are not.

- "Spiders have eight legs."
- "The sun is a planet."
- "Penguins live at the North Pole."
- "An octopus has three hearts."

Vote with your thumb: up for true, down for false.

Now check each one in a book or with your grown-up.

[CALLOUT fact] Spiders have eight legs. An octopus has three hearts. The sun is a star, not a planet. Most penguins live in the south, far from the North Pole.

[CALLOUT think] Did the sure voice make the wrong facts feel true?

[CALLOUT tip] **Level up (grades 3-5).** Write your own "sure but wrong" fact. Can your partner catch it with a book?

## Step 4: Scenario (scenario, 6 min)

**Title:** The poster fact

You are making a poster about penguins. An AI helper says, "Penguins live at the North Pole." (This story is made up.)

[SCENARIO] What would you do?

- **A. Copy it onto your poster.** Feedback: The AI sounded sure, but it was wrong. Your whole class would see the mistake.
- **B. Check a library book about penguins.** Feedback: Great choice. A good book can show you where penguins really live.
- **C. Ask your teacher.** Feedback: Also a great choice. A teacher can help you check, and find a good source.
- **D. Ask the AI the same question again.** Feedback: It might give the same wrong answer. Checking a second place works better.

## Step 5: Quick check (check, 4 min)

[CHECK] Can an AI helper be wrong?

A. No, AI is always right

B. Yes, AI can make mistakes

C. Only on weekends

D. Only if it is broken

*Answer: B. AI makes guesses, and guesses can be wrong.*

[CHECK] An AI says something in a very sure way. What does that mean?

A. It must be true

B. It is a secret

C. It might still be wrong, so check

D. The AI is happy

*Answer: C. Sounding sure is not proof. Check a book or ask a grown-up.*

[CHECK] An AI got a fact wrong for your poster. What should you do next?

A. Check a book or ask a grown-up

B. Use it anyway

C. Throw the poster away

D. Ask the AI to be quiet

*Answer: A. A second source helps you fix the mistake.*

## Step 6: Reflect (reflect, 5 min)

**Title:** A time I got a wrong answer

**What ARK thinks:** When an AI makes a mistake, people are responsible. The people who make it should fix it. The people who use it should check it.

**Some people say:** If a machine made it, nobody is to blame.

[REFLECT] What do you think? Who should fix an AI mistake?

[REFLECT] Has a person, book, or device ever told you something wrong? What did you do next?

This is just for you. Nobody else will see it.

[CALLOUT tip] **Level up (grades 3-5).** In late 2025, some self-driving cars passed school buses that had stopped. That is against the law. The company changed the software in 3,067 cars. People found the problem, and people fixed it.

[DISCUSS]

- Who should fix an AI mistake? The makers, or the users?
- When is it OK to just trust a helper? When should you always check?

| 48-52 | Step 7 recap and post-check | Whole group |

**Before you start:**

- Every activity is unplugged or led by an adult. Learners do not use AI tools themselves.
- Keep the tone light. The goal is checking, not fear of AI.
- The self-driving car example is for grades 3-5. For younger groups, skip it.
- "What ARK thinks" is an opinion. Say so out loud, and let kids disagree.

**Discussion prompts:** Use the two in Step 6.

**Answer notes:** Quick check: 1 B, 2 C, 3 A. Pre/post: 1 B, 2 B, 3 B. In Step 3, true: spiders and octopus. False: the sun (a star) and penguins (most live in the Southern Hemisphere). In the sort, accept any answer a learner can explain with "what happens if it's wrong?"
