## Objectives

By the end, learners can:

1. Say what AI is in their own words.
2. Name three AI tools people use every day.
3. Tell the difference between AI, a robot, and a regular computer program.
4. Explain that AI learns from examples, and can be fooled by something its examples never showed it.

## Vocabulary

| Term | Definition for this grade band |
| --- | --- |
| artificial intelligence (AI) | A computer program that learns from examples and then makes a guess or a choice. |
| pattern | Something that repeats, like stripes on a shirt. |
| training data | The big pile of examples an AI learns from. |
| robot | A machine that can move and do jobs. Some robots use AI. Many do not. |

## Step 1: Hook (explainer, 5 min, free preview)

**Title:** Is it smart?

A phone can finish your sentence. A TV app can pick a show you might like. A speaker can answer a question out loud.

How do they do that? Nobody is hiding inside. These tools use something called [VOCAB artificial intelligence]. We say AI for short.

AI is a computer program that learns from examples. Then it makes a guess.

[CALLOUT think] Has a phone or tablet ever guessed what you wanted? What did it guess?

In this lesson you will find out what AI is. You will also learn what it is not.

## Step 2: Core idea (explainer, 7 min)

**Title:** How AI learns

AI does not know things the way you do. It learns from examples.

Say we want an AI to spot cats. People show it thousands of cat photos. The AI looks for a [VOCAB pattern]. Pointy ears. Whiskers. A long tail.

Later it sees a new photo. It checks for those patterns. Then it guesses: cat or not a cat.

All those examples have a name. They are called [VOCAB training data].

Not every clever machine is AI. A calculator follows rules. It does not learn. A [VOCAB robot] is a machine that moves. Some robots use AI. Many do not.

[SORT] AI or not AI? | Uses AI; Does not use AI

- A voice helper that answers questions -> Uses AI -> It learned from many voices and questions.
- A calculator -> Does not use AI -> It follows the same rules every time.
- A show picker on a TV app -> Uses AI -> It learns from what people watch.
- A light switch -> Does not use AI -> It only turns on and off.
- A toy robot that walks in a line -> Does not use AI -> It moves, but it does not learn.
- Spell check that fixes your typing -> Uses AI -> It learned from lots of writing.

## Step 3: Play it (activity, 10 min)

**Title:** You are the AI

This game is unplugged. Your grown-up shows six picture cards. Each one says "apple" or "not an apple." Every apple card shows a red apple.

Now you are the AI. Look for the pattern. What makes an apple an apple?

Your grown-up holds up a new card. It is a green apple.

[CALLOUT think] Is it an apple? What would your AI say?

Your AI might say "not an apple." It only saw red apples. That is not silly. It did what its examples taught it.

Now fix your AI. Which new cards would you add to the pile?

[CALLOUT tip] **Level up (grades 3-5).** Say every dog photo an AI saw had grass in it. Then it sees a cat on grass. What might it guess? What did it really learn?

## Step 4: Scenario (scenario, 6 min)

**Title:** The drawing helper

Your class gets a new drawing app. You draw a dog. The app says, "Nice cat!"

[SCENARIO] What would you do?

- **A. Decide the app is broken and stop using it.** Feedback: It is not broken. AI guesses, and guesses can be wrong. You can keep using it and check its guesses.
- **B. Believe the app. Maybe it is a cat.** Feedback: You drew it, so you know best. AI is a helper. It does not get the final say.
- **C. Tell a grown-up and think about why it guessed wrong.** Feedback: Good thinking. Maybe the app saw few dogs like yours. Asking why is what careful AI users do.
- **D. Draw the dog again with bigger ears and see what happens.** Feedback: Nice test. Changing one thing helps you see what the AI looks for.

## Step 5: Quick check (check, 4 min)

[CHECK] What is AI?

A. A robot that walks and talks

B. A computer program that learns from examples

C. A person hiding inside a computer

D. Any machine with a screen

*Answer: B. AI is a program that learns from examples and then makes guesses.*

[CHECK] How does an AI learn to spot a cat?

A. It is born knowing

B. Someone gives it one rule

C. It looks at many cat photos and finds patterns

D. It asks a cat

*Answer: C. AI learns by finding patterns in lots of examples.*

[CHECK] Which one does NOT use AI?

A. A voice helper

B. A calculator

C. A show picker

D. Spell check

*Answer: B. A calculator follows fixed rules. It does not learn.*

[CHECK] An AI only saw red apples. It sees a green apple. What might happen?

A. It knows right away

B. It might say "not an apple"

C. It turns the apple red

D. It stops working forever

*Answer: B. AI only knows what its examples showed it.*

## Step 6: Reflect (reflect, 5 min)

**Title:** My AI helper

**What ARK thinks:** AI is a helper, not a boss. People make it. People check it. You get the final say.

**Some people say:** AI is so smart we should just trust it.

[REFLECT] What do you think? Who should get the final say, you or the AI?

[REFLECT] Think about one AI tool in your home or school. What does it help with? Has it ever guessed wrong?

This is just for you. Nobody else will see it.

[DISCUSS]

- What is one job you would never give to an AI? Why?
- If an AI guessed wrong about you, how would you fix it?

## Step 7: Recap (recap, 3 min)

[RECAP]

- AI is a computer program that learns from examples.
- AI looks for patterns and then makes a guess.
- AI only knows what its examples showed it. So it can guess wrong.
- Robots and calculators are not always AI.

## Facilitator guide

**Materials:** Picture cards for Step 3: three red apples labeled "apple," three red things labeled "not an apple" (a ball, a strawberry, a tomato), and one green apple with no label. Signs for the Step 2 sort. Paper for drawing.

**Timing for a 45-60 minute session:**

| Minutes | What happens | Format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0-3 | Pre-check, read aloud | Whole group |
| 3-8 | Step 1: "Has a device ever guessed?" | Whole group |
| 8-18 | Step 2 and the sort as a stand-up game | Whole group |
| 18-28 | Step 3 Play it: You are the AI | Small groups |
| 28-35 | Step 4 scenario vote | Whole group |
| 35-40 | Step 5 quick check | Whole group |
| 40-47 | Step 6: What ARK thinks, then reflect | Individual, then pairs |
| 47-52 | Step 7 recap and post-check | Whole group |

**Before you start:**

- Every activity is unplugged or led by an adult. Learners do not use AI tools themselves.
- For grades K-2, skip the Level up box and act out Step 3 with real fruit if you can.
- For grades 3-5, use the Level up box and ask learners to invent their own "tricky card."
- "What ARK thinks" is an opinion. Say so out loud, and let kids disagree.

**Discussion prompts:** Use the two in Step 6.

**Answer notes:** Quick check: 1 B, 2 C, 3 B, 4 B. Pre/post: 1 C, 2 B, 3 A. In Step 3, the strawberry and tomato are good tricky cards: red, round, and not apples.
